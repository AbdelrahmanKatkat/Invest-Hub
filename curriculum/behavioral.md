# Behavioral Finance — curriculum spec

**End state:** recognise anchoring, overconfidence, mental accounting and the asymmetry between gains and losses, and
know practical defenses. **Status:** Module 0 is a basic pass; extends Investment Fundamentals Lesson 0.9's intro to
loss aversion, recency bias and herding.

## Module 0 · Foundations  (`behavioral-0.html`)
| id | Lesson | Must understand | Real-world link |
|---|---|---|---|
| anchoring | Anchoring & framing | An irrelevant starting number can still skew a judgement; how a choice is framed changes the decision | A "was $100, now $70" sale price anchoring perceived value |
| overconfidence | Overconfidence | People are often more certain of their own judgement than their actual accuracy justifies | Investors overestimating their ability to time the market |
| mental-accounting | Mental accounting | Money is treated differently depending on its mental "label" (salary vs. a windfall), even though it spends the same | Spending a tax refund more freely than equivalent salary income |
| prospect-theory | Prospect theory | Losses are commonly felt more strongly than equivalent gains, and people treat the two differently near a reference point | Why a drawdown feels worse than an equivalent earlier gain felt good |
| debiasing-techniques | Debiasing techniques | Checklists, rules written in advance, and automation reduce the room for in-the-moment biased decisions | Investment policy statements and automated contributions (Investment Fundamentals Module 4) |
