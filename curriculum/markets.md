# Financial Markets — curriculum spec

**End state:** describe the main kinds of markets, how they're structured, the case for and against market efficiency,
why markets are regulated, and how trading spans the globe. **Status:** Module 0 is a deep pass. Module 1 (Investment
Fundamentals) already covers order types, spreads, a single exchange's mechanics and settlement at a beginner level —
this path goes wider across market types rather than repeating that ground.

## Module 0 · Foundations  (`markets-0.html`)
| id | Lesson | Must understand | Real-world link |
|---|---|---|---|
| market-types | Market types | Equity, bond, currency (FX) and derivatives markets as distinct arenas with different participants and purposes | Stock exchanges, the bond market, the FX market, futures exchanges |
| exchange-vs-otc | Exchange vs. OTC | A centralized exchange matches orders publicly; over-the-counter trading is negotiated directly between parties | NYSE/Nasdaq (exchange) vs. most bonds and many derivatives (OTC) |
| market-efficiency | Market efficiency | The efficient market hypothesis (weak/semi-strong/strong forms) and why consistently beating a fair-priced market is hard | Index funds' popularity partly rests on an efficiency-based argument |
| regulators-and-oversight | Regulators & oversight | Why markets are regulated (disclosure, fraud prevention, fairness) and what a regulator typically does | National securities regulators overseeing exchanges and listed companies |
| globalization-of-markets | Globalization of markets | Markets trade nearly around the clock as different time zones open and close; capital flows across borders | Asian markets closing as European markets open, overlapping with US hours |
